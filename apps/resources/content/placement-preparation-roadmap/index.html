Placement Preparation Roadmap · 2026 02
Phase 01 · Foundation — Days 1–30

Build the Base Before You Sprint

1
Step 1 · Know where you stand
Audit Your Current Standing
Before planning anything, get honest numbers: how many DSA problems can you solve unaided, does your resume have a real project, and how strong are your core CS fundamentals. Write these down — this baseline is what every later phase measures progress against. Most wasted prep time comes from skipping this and jumping straight to "grinding."
Start here Sets your baseline
Rate yourself on DSA, projects, CS core Write down honest gaps, not guesses
2
Step 2 · The non-negotiables
Master Programming Fundamentals
Pick one primary language (Java, C++, or Python) and get genuinely fluent in it — syntax should never slow you down in an interview. Alongside this, build a working understanding of OOP concepts, since almost every interviewer probes them regardless of role.
One language, deep fluency OOP fundamentals
Pick one language and commit OOP: classes, inheritance, polymorphism
3
Step 3 · Where DSA actually starts
DSA — Arrays, Strings & Basic Patterns
Start with arrays, strings, hashing, and basic recursion — this is where most placement DSA rounds pull their easy-to-medium questions from. Aim for consistency over volume: 2–3 problems a day, solved and understood, beats a 20-problem binge you forget by next week.
Consistency over volume Arrays, strings, hashing
2–3 problems a day, every day Understand the pattern, not just the answer
4
Step 4 · Your first impression on paper
Build Your Resume Skeleton
Draft a one-page, ATS-friendly resume with clear sections for education, skills, and projects — even if a project slot is a placeholder for now. Use this early draft to identify exactly what's missing, so Phase 2's project work fills a real gap instead of being built blindly.
One page, ATS-friendly Reveals your gaps early
One page, no filler bullet points Use it to find missing pieces
5
Step 5 · Where recruiters actually look
Set Up Your Public Profile
Get GitHub, LinkedIn, and a coding profile (LeetCode or GeeksforGeeks) in order before you need them under pressure. A clean GitHub with even 2–3 real repos, a complete LinkedIn with a clear headline, and an active coding profile all signal seriousness before a recruiter reads a single resume line.
Do this once, well GitHub · LinkedIn · Coding profile
2–3 real repos, not empty forks Complete LinkedIn headline and about
🎯
Phase 1 is about removing excuses, not achieving mastery. By day 30 you should have a working resume, a public profile that doesn't embarrass you, and a daily DSA habit — not perfection. Perfection comes from repetition in the phases ahead, not from getting Phase 1 flawless.

Placement Preparation Roadmap · 2026 03
Phase 02 · Core Build — Days 31–60

Where Projects and Problem-Solving Compound

6
Step 6 · The interview-round staples
DSA — Intermediate Patterns
Move into two pointers, sliding window, recursion, backtracking, and basic sorting/searching variations. These patterns cover the majority of medium-difficulty questions asked in actual placement rounds — recognize the pattern first, then write the code, rather than memorizing solutions.
Pattern-first, not memorized Two pointers · Sliding window · Backtracking
Identify the pattern before coding Re-solve without looking after 1 week
7
Step 7 · The resume centerpiece
Build Your Flagship Project
Build one real, deployed, fullstack project — not a tutorial clone — that solves an actual problem, however small. Interviewers can tell the difference between a project you followed along to and one you built decisions for. Deploy it, write a clear README, and be ready to explain every choice you made.
Deployed, not just coded Be ready to defend every choice
Solve a real problem, however small Deploy it and write a real README
8
Step 8 · Beyond just coding
Learn System Design Basics
For most fresher and intern roles, this means low-level design — clean class structure, basic design patterns — plus a light introduction to high-level concepts like scalability, caching, and database choice. You don't need to design a distributed system yet; you need to explain your project's architecture clearly.
LLD first, HLD lightly Explain your own project's design
Clean class structure and patterns Know why you chose your DB and stack
9
Step 9 · What every core round tests
Core CS Subjects Deep Dive
DBMS (normalization, joins, indexing), OS (processes, threads, memory management), and CN (OSI/TCP layers, HTTP basics) show up in almost every core technical round. Build a one-page cheat sheet per subject covering the questions asked most often, and revisit it weekly rather than cramming once.
DBMS · OS · CN One-page cheat sheet per subject
Weekly revisits beat one-time cramming Focus on the most commonly asked topics
10
Step 10 · Visible proof of skill
Contribute to Open Source or Build in Public
Even a handful of merged pull requests or a public build-log of your flagship project adds credibility that a resume line alone can't. This doesn't need to be a major open-source project — fixing documentation, small bugs, or posting weekly progress updates all count and show initiative.
Optional but high-signal Small contributions count
Even small PRs add credibility Public build-logs show initiative
🛠️
Phase 2 is where most students either build momentum or quietly fall behind. Your project and your DSA habit should be running in parallel, not sequentially — don't wait to "finish DSA" before starting the project, or you'll run out of runway before Phase 3.
DSA Coverage — Week by Week
Weeks Focus Topics Target Outcome
Week 1–2 Arrays, strings, hashing, basic recursion Comfortable with easy-level problems unaided
Week 3–4 Sorting, searching, two pointers, sliding window Recognize the pattern within the first read
Week 5–6 Linked lists, stacks, queues, backtracking Solve medium problems in under 25 minutes
Week 7–8 Trees, graphs (BFS/DFS), intro to DP Ready to move into Phase 3's advanced set

Placement Preparation Roadmap · 2026 04
Phase 03 · Interview Ready — Days 61–75

Turning Practice Into Interview Performance

11
Step 11 · The final DSA push
Advanced DSA — Trees, Graphs & Dynamic Programming
This is where placement rounds actually differentiate candidates: tree traversals and manipulation, graph algorithms (BFS/DFS, shortest paths), and dynamic programming (knapsack-style, longest subsequence patterns). Don't chase every DP variant — master the 15–20 patterns that repeat most often across companies.
Where candidates differentiate Trees · Graphs · DP
Master 15–20 repeating DP patterns Practice explaining your approach aloud
12
Step 12 · Practice under real pressure
Mock Technical Interviews
Solving problems alone on a screen is a different skill from solving them out loud, under time pressure, while someone watches. Do at least 3 mock interviews — with peers, seniors, or a platform — before your first real one, and record yourself at least once to catch habits you can't see from the inside.
At least 3 mocks Record yourself once
Talk through your thinking out loud Review the recording for filler habits
13
Step 13 · The HR round groundwork
Prepare STAR Stories for the HR Round
Write out 4–5 real stories from your projects, teamwork, or coursework using the STAR structure — Situation, Task, Action, Result — that can flex across multiple common HR questions (conflict, failure, leadership, pressure). Prepare the shape, not a memorized script, so you sound natural instead of rehearsed.
STAR structure Shape, not a script
4–5 flexible stories from real experience Practice the shape, never a script
14
Step 14 · Know your own work cold
Revise System Design & Project Deep-Dive Answers
Be ready to explain your flagship project's architecture, the trade-offs you made, and what you'd do differently with more time — this is one of the most common technical-round segments. Prepare answers for "why this database," "how would this scale," and "what was the hardest bug you hit."
Common technical segment Trade-offs and scale questions
"Why this database" and "how would it scale" Prepare your hardest-bug story
15
Step 15 · Precision over volume
Resume Tailoring Per Company or Role
Adjust your resume's emphasis — which projects lead, which skills are listed first — to match each company's stated stack and role, rather than sending one static resume everywhere. This takes 10 minutes per application and meaningfully improves how relevant you look to an ATS and a human reader alike.
10 minutes per application Match their stack, not yours
Reorder skills to match the JD Lead with the most relevant project
⭐
By the end of Phase 3, nothing in an interview should catch you off guard. You should be able to walk through your project, solve a medium DSA problem while narrating your thought process, and answer a behavioral question — all without needing a script in front of you.

Phase 04 · Application & Offer — Days 76–90

Executing the Final Sprint

16
Step 16 · Widen the funnel
Apply Strategically
Don't rely on campus placements alone — build a target list across on-campus drives, off-campus applications, and referrals through seniors or LinkedIn. Referrals meaningfully increase response rates, so it's worth spending real time building those connections before this phase, not scrambling for them in it.
On-campus + off-campus + referrals Build a real target list
Don't rely on campus drives alone Referrals meaningfully help response rates
17
Step 17 · The first filter
Ace the Online Assessment Round
Practice under actual test conditions — a timer, no notes, a proctored-style environment — since OA platforms differ from casual problem-solving. Know the common OA question types for your target companies in advance (aptitude, DSA, sometimes SQL) so nothing on the day itself is a surprise.
Practice under real conditions Know the format in advance
Timed, no-notes practice sessions Research each company's OA format
18
Step 18 · Where preparation shows
Technical Interview Rounds — 1 to 3
Expect a mix of live DSA, project deep-dives, and core CS questions across multiple rounds. Think out loud in every round — interviewers evaluate your reasoning process at least as much as your final answer, and silent thinking gives them nothing to evaluate.
Think out loud, always DSA + project + CS core mix
Narrate your reasoning, not just the code Ask clarifying questions before diving in
19
Step 19 · The human filter
HR / Behavioral Round
Deploy the STAR stories built in Phase 3 here — this round tests self-awareness, communication, and culture fit more than raw skill. Always come with 2–3 thoughtful questions for the interviewer; having none is one of the most common reasons candidates lose points at this stage.
Deploy your STAR stories Always ask 2–3 questions back
Use the stories you already prepared Have real questions ready for them
20
Step 20 · Closing it out
Negotiate & Evaluate the Offer
Research market compensation for the role, level, and location before any negotiation conversation, and evaluate the full offer — role, team, growth path, and compensation — not just the number on the letter. It's fine to ask for time to review before accepting; a rushed decision here outlasts the 90-day plan by years.
Research before negotiating Evaluate the whole offer, not just pay
Research market range before responding It's fine to ask for time to decide
📩
Applications are a numbers game with a quality floor. A tailored application to 30 well-matched companies will consistently outperform a generic blast to 200 — the goal in Phase 4 is targeted volume, not maximum volume.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Where Otherwise-Strong Candidates Lose the Most Ground

🚩 Tutorial-hopping without finishing anything
Starting a new course or project every time momentum dips leaves you with ten half-finished things and nothing to show. Finish the flagship project before starting a second one.
🚩 DSA without revision
Solving a problem once and moving on feels productive but doesn't build recall. Revisit problems you struggled with after a week — that's where retention actually happens.
🚩 Ignoring core CS subjects
DSA-only preparation leaves you exposed in core technical rounds, where DBMS, OS, and CN questions are extremely common and easy to prepare for in advance.
🚩 Skipping mock interviews
Solving problems silently on LeetCode is a different skill from explaining your thinking out loud under time pressure. Mocks are where you find that gap before a real interviewer does.
Suggested Weekly Time Allocation
Activity Phase 1–2 Focus Phase 3–4 Focus
DSA Practice 40% of prep time 30% of prep time
Project Work 30% of prep time 10% of prep time
Core CS Subjects 20% of prep time 15% of prep time
Mock Interviews & HR Prep 10% of prep time 35% of prep time
Applications & Outreach — 10% of prep time
⏱️
The ratio shifts, the daily habit doesn't. DSA practice never drops to zero, even in the final sprint — a short daily session keeps your pattern recognition sharp right up to interview day.

Daily & Weekly Prep Routine

The Habit Structure Behind the Whole Roadmap

🟥 Every Morning (45–60 min)
2–3 DSA problems, timed
Review yesterday's tough problem
Log progress in a simple tracker
🟧 Every Evening (60–90 min)
Project work or core CS revision
Read one interview experience post
Update resume or profile if needed
🟩 Every Weekend
Full revision of the week's DSA topics
One mock interview or peer review
Plan next week's target companies
90-Day Roadmap Checklist
Before You Move to the Next Phase
Resume finalized and tailored, GitHub and LinkedIn profiles complete
Comfortable solving easy-to-medium DSA problems across arrays, strings, trees, and graphs
One flagship project fully built, deployed, and ready to explain end-to-end
4–5 STAR stories prepared and rehearsed out loud, not just written down
Core CS cheat sheets (DBMS, OS, CN) reviewed at least three times
At least 3 mock interviews completed, with one recorded and reviewed
Target company list built with a mix of on-campus, off-campus, and referral routes
🔁
This roadmap is a loop, not a straight line. If interviews don't land immediately after Phase 4, cycle back into DSA revision and mock interviews rather than starting from scratch — most successful placements come after more than one attempt.
